Paper Planner

A paper-style planner for Windows touchscreen PCs and Android tablets. Write on the pages with your pen, just like a paper day planner.

Installing on Windows

  1. Open the downloaded PaperPlanner-1.17.1.msi.
  2. Windows may say "Windows protected your PC", because the app is new and not from a big publisher. Click More info, then Run anyway.
  3. It installs just for you, with no administrator password, and adds Paper Planner to the Start menu and desktop.
  4. Optional: handwriting search needs Microsoft's free .NET 10 Desktop Runtime (x64). Everything else works without it.
  5. Optional: to see your Google Calendar, choose ⋮ → Show Google Calendar… in the app. Google may warn that it "hasn't verified this app". Choose Advanced → Go to Paper Planner.

Installing on an Android tablet

It works best on a tablet with a pen, such as a Samsung Galaxy Tab with S Pen.

  1. Open this page on the tablet and tap Download for Android tablets.
  2. Open the downloaded PaperPlanner-0.31.apk (from the browser's downloads, or in My Files → Downloads).
  3. When asked, allow your browser or My Files to install unknown apps, then go back and tap Install. Google Play Protect may ask to scan the app first, which is fine.
  4. The tablet version shows the calendars already on your tablet (allow calendar access when asked). There's no separate Google sign-in.

To update either version, install the new one over the old one. Your planner is kept. On the tablet, uninstalling deletes your planner, so use ⋮ → Back up notes… first.

What it does

  • Year, month, week and day pages that look and feel like a paper planner.
  • Write with a pen or type. Turn pages and zoom with your fingers.
  • Search your own handwriting. Recognition runs on your own device.
  • Optionally shows events from your Google Calendar on the pages, and lets you add events to it.
  • Optionally keeps a PC and a tablet in step through your own personal OneDrive.

Google Calendar

Connecting Google Calendar on the PC is optional. When you connect it, Paper Planner asks Google for permission to:

  • see the list of your calendars, so you can choose which ones appear in the planner, and
  • see, add and change events, so your events show on the right pages and you can add new events from the planner.

On the PC, your calendar goes directly between your PC and Google. On a tablet, the app uses the calendars already on the tablet. It is never sent to the developer or anyone else. See the privacy policy.
